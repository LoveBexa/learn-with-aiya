Beyond the prompt: what actually changes after week one

Most people arrive thinking AI is a smarter search box. They leave having shipped something small, useful and theirs.

The first question we hear is almost always the same: “Am I going to break something?” The second is: “Do I need to know how to code?” By hour three, neither question matters much anymore.

That’s not because we hand out confidence like stickers. It’s because the room is designed for small wins — a working draft, a live link, a workflow that saves twenty minutes on a task you already hate.

The shift isn’t technical first

Beginners don’t get stuck on models or APIs. They get stuck on permission — the feeling that AI is for “other people.” Week one is about reversing that. You sit next to someone who asked the same question two minutes ago. You watch a tutor unstick them in plain English. You realise the gap is smaller than LinkedIn made it look.

“I thought I’d leave with notes. I left with something I could send to a client.”

What we actually build

Nothing precious. A one-page site. A simple assistant tuned to your voice. An automation that pulls the boring bit out of a weekly report. The point isn’t the artefact — it’s the repeatable loop: idea → prompt → tweak → ship → show someone.

Once you’ve run that loop once, with humans in the room, the second time is yours. That’s the whole thesis of AIYA: beyond the prompt, into the build.
